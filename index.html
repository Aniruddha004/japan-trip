      Guides: Suica, cash, eSIM and departure from India

      💳 Suica / IC card

      Suica isn’t an unlimited pass. It’s a prepaid tap card: you load yen and each ride deducts its fare. It works on the metro, JR local trains, private railways and buses in Tokyo and Kansai, and pays at convenience stores, vending machines and lockers. Your IC legs from 27 Nov to 6 Dec add up to about — per person (— without Kamakura).

      Your pick with the Scapia card: TOURIST PASMO (any phone)

      The visitor version of PASMO, Suica’s twin card. It works everywhere Suica does, and needs no app.

      1. After customs at HND T3, go to the 2F arrival lobby. Use the travellers’ ticket machine next to the Keikyu Tourist Information Center, beside the Keikyu ticket gates, or ask at the counter.
      2. Choose ¥10,000 (options run ¥1,000–10,000) and pay with your Scapia Visa, with no forex markup. That covers most of your fares; one ¥5,000 cash top-up later should see you through.
      3. If you’ll meet Divyank at Narita, buy his card at the same time. Otherwise he can buy one at the Skyliner & Keisei Information Center in the Narita T1/T2 station for ¥2,000.
      4. It’s valid for 28 days, with no deposit and no refunds. Keep the reference paper it comes with.
      5. Top-ups are cash only. They’re free at 7-Eleven ATMs (“Recharge E-money”, ¥1,000 steps) or at any station ticket machine, up to a maximum balance of ¥20,000.

      Alternative · iPhone: Welcome Suica Mobile (JR East app)

      • Needs iOS 17.2 or later. You issue a Suica in the app with a “secret keyword”, with no deposit or fee.
      • Top-ups work only through Apple Pay. Apple’s India page names Axis Bank cards, not Federal Bank, so your Scapia card probably won’t add to Wallet, but your Flipkart Axis card should. Each top-up then costs about 3% extra (Axis’s 3.5% foreign fee plus GST, minus 1% cashback).
      • The balance expires 180 days after issue and can’t be refunded. Set it as the Express Transit card and carry the power bank.

      Using it

      • Tap in and out at every gate. On buses, tap the reader as signed (Kyoto city buses are a flat ¥230).
      • Short on balance at the exit? Top up at the fare-adjustment machine beside the gates.
      • Paid separately: Shinkansen (Smart EX), Romancecar seat fees, highway buses, and the Hakone ropeway and cruise (Freepass).
      • Spend what’s left at a convenience store or the airport before you fly home.

      💴 Cash and cards (Scapia Visa)

      Your Scapia Federal credit card charges no forex markup abroad, so pay by card wherever Japan takes it. Keep cash for what doesn’t take cards: many temple counters, market stalls, small eateries and IC top-ups.

      How much yen to carry

      IC top-up after the ¥10,000 loaded by card¥5,000
      Temple and castle entry (8–9 paid sights, often cash-only)¥6,000–8,000
      Street food, market stalls, small eateries (≈ ¥1,000 a day)¥12,000–15,000
      Lockers, charms and small extras¥2,000–3,000
      Carry per person¥30,000
      For the two of you¥60,000 (≈ —)

      Getting yen in India

      1. Order 2–3 days before you fly from any forex-enabled branch of your bank in the city you’re in (it doesn’t have to be your home branch), or from an RBI-authorised money changer (many take online orders and deliver to your door). Branches don’t always stock yen, and airport counters have the worst rates.
      2. Take your passport, Japan visa, air ticket and PAN card. RBI makes PAN mandatory for every forex purchase.
      3. Compare two or three quotes. They’re usually per ¥100: the reference is about ₹59.4 per ¥100, and dealers add a few percent on top.
      4. Pay by UPI or bank transfer (cash is fine below ₹50,000). Expect a small GST line of about ₹65.
      5. For ¥60,000, ask for 4 × ¥5,000 and 40 × ¥1,000. Stalls and temple counters are easier with small notes than with ¥10,000s.
      6. Count the notes on delivery. Both the old and the new (2024) designs are valid in Japan.

      In Japan

      • Pay with the Scapia card (tap or insert) at convenience stores, restaurants, hotels, USJ, Skytree, shops and taxis. If a terminal offers to charge in rupees, choose yen, because the rupee option adds the terminal’s own markup.
      • Need more cash? Use your Axis debit card at a 7-Eleven ATM (also at HND and NRT). Visa and Mastercard work 24 h; a RuPay card only works if it also carries a JCB or Discover logo. Axis charges ₹125 per withdrawal plus a 3.5% markup, with GST on top: about ₹640 (≈ 5%) on ¥20,000, plus 7-Eleven’s own fee shown on screen. Take ¥20,000–30,000 at a time (maximum ¥100,000).
      • At the ATM step “select the currency used to calculate your billing amount”, choose Japanese yen. Rupees adds the ATM’s conversion rate plus Axis’s separate 3.5% conversion fee.
      • Avoid the Scapia credit card at ATMs: it counts as a cash advance, with a fee per its MITC, interest from the day you withdraw, and no rewards.
      • Keep the Flipkart Axis credit card as a backup only. Axis adds a 3.5% foreign transaction fee plus GST (≈ 4.1%), and the same 3.5% on rupee payments to merchants registered abroad, against just 1% cashback. At ATMs it’s a cash advance: 2.5% (minimum ₹500) plus GST, and 3.75% a month interest from day one.
      • Spend your coins before you leave: money changers in India buy back notes, not coins. You can sell back leftover notes or keep them for a future trip.

      Before you fly (Scapia app)

      • Turn on international usage and online payments, and set the card PIN (needed at ATMs and some chip terminals).
      • In the Axis Mobile app, switch on international ATM use for your debit card, as your cash backup. It’s off by default.
      • Keep your Indian SIM reachable for OTPs on online bookings (Smart EX, the Freepass, buses).
      • Save Federal Bank’s helpline for card problems abroad, +91-484-6925000, and carry a second card as a backup.

      📶 eSIM from India (both phones)

      A data-only Japan eSIM is usually much cheaper per GB than an Indian roaming pack. Set it up at home and switch it on when you land.

      Before you fly

      1. Check the phone supports eSIM and is unlocked. iPhone: Settings → General → About → “Carrier Lock” should say No SIM restrictions. Android: Settings → Connections → SIM manager should offer “Add eSIM”.
      2. Buy a Japan data plan valid for at least 15 days (24 Nov–7 Dec). Around 1 GB a day covers maps, translation and photos. Compare the price per GB, validity, hotspot use and which Japanese network it runs on (docomo, au or SoftBank).
      3. Pay with your Scapia card (no forex markup) after switching on international online use in the Scapia app. Other Indian cards usually add a 2–3.5% markup.
      4. Install it on home Wi-Fi from the QR code or the provider’s app. Label it “Japan”, keep your Indian SIM as the default for calls and SMS, and switch the Japan line off until you land. Don’t delete it: many eSIMs can’t be reinstalled.
      5. Turn on international roaming for calls and SMS on your Indian number so bank OTPs still reach you. Ask your operator what incoming SMS costs abroad.

      On landing at HND

      1. Switch the Japan line on, choose it for mobile data, and turn Data Roaming on for that line.
      2. Turn data roaming off on the Indian SIM, and turn off “Allow mobile data switching”, to avoid rupee charges.
      3. Test Google Maps. No data? Toggle Airplane mode, then check the provider’s APN settings.

      Data-only eSIMs don’t include a Japanese phone number, so use WhatsApp or FaceTime for calls.

      🛫 Departure day in India (Mon 23 Nov)

      You land at HND T3 at 04:55 on Tue 24 Nov, so you leave India on the evening of the 23rd.

      Before leaving home

      • Web check-in (usually opens 24–48 h before) and the boarding pass on your phone.
      • Documents: passport, Japan visa (for an eVISA, have the visa issuance notice ready on your phone as your eVISA email instructs), e-ticket, first hotel booking, return ticket and insurance.
      • Money: ¥30,000 each in cash (see “Cash and cards”), and the Scapia card with international use on. RBI limits: up to USD 3,000-equivalent in foreign notes per trip, and up to ₹25,000 in rupees.
      • Cabin bag: power bank and spare batteries (never in checked bags), liquids of 100 ml or less in one 1-litre clear bag, and medicines in their original packs with the prescription. Japan bans some cold and allergy medicines with stimulants (such as pseudoephedrine) and limits codeine, so check before packing.
      • Visit Japan Web: finish the immigration and customs steps and screenshot the QR code.

      At the airport (arrive 3 h before departure)

      1. Terminal entry: show your passport and ticket at the entry gate.
      2. Check-in and bag drop (counters usually close 60–75 min before departure): show your passport and visa, and check the bag tags say HND.
      3. Optional customs stop: declare high-value items you’re taking out (camera, laptop, jewellery) at the customs desk before immigration, so they aren’t questioned when you return.
      4. Emigration: Indian-passport counters, or the e-gates if you’re enrolled in Fast Track Immigration (FTI-TTP). No departure card is needed.
      5. Security: laptop, tablet, liquids and power bank out unless told otherwise.
      6. Airside: eat and refill water, then be at the gate 45 min before departure (boarding usually closes 20–25 min before).

      Last half-hour on airport Wi-Fi

      • Offline Google Maps for Tokyo, the HND T3 → hotel route saved, and Smart EX and GO logged in.
      • Indian SIM: data roaming off. Japan eSIM: installed and still switched off.
      • On board: set your watch to JST (IST + 3 h 30 min) and sleep, because the day starts at 04:55.
